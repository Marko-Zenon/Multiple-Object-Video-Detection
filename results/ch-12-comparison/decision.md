## 4. Resolution sensitivity, reported separately (rule 5)

`yolo_imgsz1600` (CH-10's selection under its own rule) reaches 0.1710 val_full mAP. That is +0.027 over the CH-09 baseline
(4.3x the spread), at 49.7 vs 75.2 FPS in the common scope and about 1.42x the pixels. It is **resolution-sensitivity evidence for
YOLO11s only**:
- it stays 0.0866 below RT-DETR-l at 1344;
- RT-DETR-l has no result at 1600;
- RT-DETR-l stretches frames to a square `imgsz x imgsz`, so 1600 would also cost about 1.42x per step.

Under rule 5 the final input size therefore stays **1344**. A 1600 RT-DETR-l run would need its own evidence. That is a possible
CH-13 inference-size experiment (CH-13 owns inference size), not a training change frozen here.

## 5. Practical trade-offs

- **Accuracy vs speed** (`figures/accuracy_speed.png`): YOLO11s is about 4-5x faster than RT-DETR-l in the common scope
  (55-75 vs 13.9 FPS) and has 3.5x fewer parameters. It is 0.095 mAP lower at 1344, and still 0.087 lower at 1600.
  Faster R-CNN and RetinaNet are both slower *and* less accurate than RT-DETR-l in the common scope.
- **Training cost** (`summary.csv` `train_seconds`, RTX 5070, 16,800 images):
  - RT-DETR-l 4,056-4,289 s;
  - torchvision 2,020-3,098 s at batch 2;
  - YOLO11s 1,568-1,935 s.
  RT-DETR-l is the most expensive family to train, and `final.json` is sized from its measured rate.
- **Detection cap**: RT-DETR-l returns at most 300 boxes per image (its 300 queries), and every val_full frame hits that cap
  (CH-11 `query_saturation.json`). Raising the queries to 500 lowered mAP (0.2389).

## 6. Training-data and schedule choice (rule 4)

- **Why more distinct frames.**
  - `rtdetr_baseline` peaks on val_fast at epoch 3 (0.2691) and ends at 0.2617 (`curves.csv`). More passes over the same
    2,800 frames are therefore not supported.
  - The remaining lever inside the budget is more distinct frames. plan.md CH-14 already plans to measure that data-strategy
    gain against this rubric model.
- **Why every 4th frame.**
  - Adjacent frames are near-duplicates: frames 2 apart share 99.1% of their targets at a median same-track IoU of 0.81
    (`results/ch-06-eda/tables/redundancy.csv`).
  - At a gap of 4 the median IoU drops to 0.66, so the extra frames show the same tracks at clearly different positions.
  - `train_rubric` already covers 98.6% of the tracks (`subsampling.csv`), so the added value is new views, not new objects.
  - Every 4th frame gives 6,072 frames (2.17x), and its class mix matches the full train set within 0.1 pp. `train_rubric`
    over-represents person by 4.7 pp (`subsampling_class_mix.csv`).
  - Every 2nd frame (12,114) would cost about 5 h for mostly IoU-0.81 duplicates. All frames (24,201) would take about 10 h and
    would not fit the window with a buffer.
- **Why 6 epochs.** This is the validated schedule shape. Ultralytics counts warmup in epochs (3), so the warmup fraction and
  the linear decay stay as validated.
- **Unchanged settings**, each with its evidence:
  - 300 queries: q500 was worse.
  - Auto learning rate: lr0 1e-4 was worse; the confounded run is not used.
  - Flip-only augmentation: no other augmentation was tested.
  - 1344: section 4.
- **Runtime** (`final.json`): from 4,138 s / 16,800 images (0.2463 s/image, including the per-epoch overhead), 36,432 images is
  about 2.5 h. With export (4 min), val_full (5 min), test inference (7 min) and a one-resume buffer it is about 3.3 h.
  Starting by Mon 22:00 at the latest, it ends by about Tue 01:20, inside the Tue 08:00 / 12:00 cut-offs.
  Peak memory is 3.55e9 bytes at batch 1 on the 8 GB card (G9).
- **CH-14 note**: `scripts/train_ultra.py` reads `train_rubric_manifest.json` from the data folder. CH-14 must generalise it to
  the `train_final` manifest and smoke-test it before the full run (`final.json` `ch14_implementation_note`).

## 7. Speed: hardware and scope separation

`speed_same_session/summary.json`: one RTX 5070 Laptop session, 3 interleaved rounds, fresh process per call, median. Two scopes:

| model | common scope FPS (FP32, host BGR array, incl. transfer + resize + NMS) | native torchvision scope FPS (AMP, GPU-resident tensors) | FPS stored in the run's own speed.json |
|---|---|---|---|
| Faster R-CNN RPN 3000 | 9.1 | 22.5 | 14.85 (other session) |
| RetinaNet anchors | 6.3 | 30.9 | 19.71 (other session) |
| YOLO11s + P2 | 55.4 | — | 53.8 |
| YOLO11s 1600 | 49.7 | — | 49.3 |
| YOLO11s baseline | 75.2 | — | **36.37 on the RTX 3050** (not comparable) |
| RT-DETR-l | 13.9 | — | 13.8 (CH-11 session: 10.6) |

- Only numbers in the same column of this table are comparable.
- Torchvision speed depends strongly on scope: in FP32 RetinaNet is slower than Faster R-CNN, under AMP it is faster.
- Absolute FPS also shift between sessions: the same Faster R-CNN checkpoint gave 14.85 at CH-07 time and 22.5 here, in the
  same scope.

- The figures show individual frames. Their interpretation is the students'.

## 9. Limitations

- One seed per configuration. The spread comes from one replicate pair on two GPUs.
- val_fast is a frame subset of val_full.
- Framework defaults differ: optimizer, batch, RT-DETR's square resize vs letterbox.
- The CH-09 YOLO baseline was trained on another GPU.
- The data-strategy gain of `final.json` is untested until CH-14 measures it. The rubric checkpoint is the fallback.
